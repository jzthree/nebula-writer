# Science

Measured on 40 recent open-access Science research articles; numbers are per
paragraph unless stated.

## House conventions

- **American spelling**: analyze, behavior, color, center, labeled, modeling,
  signaling, tumor.
- Approximations with "~" (~40%, ~3 kb) or "approximately"; "about" is rare.
- "e.g.," and "i.e.," are used in parentheses and in text; "for instance" also
  appears.
- "likely" rather than "probably".
- Figure references with **capital panel letters**: (Fig. 2B), (Fig. 2, B and C),
  (Fig. 3, A to D); supplementary items lowercase: (fig. S4), (fig. S4, A and B),
  (table S2), (movie S3). Main and supplementary are combined with a semicolon:
  (Fig. 4A; fig. S7).
- Statistics compact: P < 0.01 or p=0.003, (n=8 mice). Thousands with commas.
- Semicolons and colons are used more than in the Nature journals (about one
  semicolon per 300 words); em dashes are rare.
- Abbreviations defined at first use in parentheses.

## Section rules

**Abstract**, ~125 words, ~6 sentences of ~22 words, **no citations**.
Background in one or two sentences, what was unknown ("remain elusive",
"is unclear"), then "Here, we …" (Science often keeps the comma), two or
three sentences of findings, one sentence of significance. Short, plain,
almost no parentheses.

**Introduction**, ~140 words, ~6 sentences of ~25 words, the most heavily
cited prose (about four citation groups per paragraph, often several refs
per claim). Science research articles often open their first section with
the context and close the introduction with "Here, we …" and a figure
reference to the overview panel (Fig. 1A).

**Results**, ~150 words, ~7 sentences averaging 24 words with the widest
spread of any journal (short declaratives next to long ones). About five
parentheses per paragraph, mostly figure panels. Paragraphs often open with
the purpose ("To investigate whether …, we …", "To determine …") or with
"We next …", "We first …", and close with a one-sentence conclusion of that
experiment ("Thus, …", "Together, the data are consistent with …",
"These results indicate that …"). "In contrast" and "Next" are common;
"Moreover", "Furthermore", "Additionally", "Notably" appear but less than
once per two paragraphs combined.

**Discussion**, ~145 words, ~6 sentences of ~26 words, almost no
parentheses, the most hedged section of all four journals (about two hedges
per paragraph: "may", "likely", "suggest", "could"). Opens with "Here, we
have shown …" or a restatement of the advance; later paragraphs set the
result against prior models with citations and end on implications or open
questions.

**Methods (Materials and Methods)**, ~135 words, ~6 sentences of ~24 words;
the most passive of the four journals (about five passive constructions per
paragraph) and rarely "we". Catalog numbers, concentrations, temperatures
and software versions in parentheses.

## Tone

Compact and declarative. Each Results paragraph is one experiment with its
question, method in one clause, outcome with numbers and panels, and a
one-line conclusion. Claims are scoped carefully; the Discussion hedges
more than the Nature journals do.
