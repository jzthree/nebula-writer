# Nature Genetics

Measured on 40 recent open-access Nature Genetics research articles; numbers
are per paragraph unless stated.

## House conventions

- **American spelling** (Springer Nature's US-edited title): analyze, behavior,
  color, labeled, modeling, signaling, tumor.
- Approximations with "~" (~2.5-fold, ~40%); "about" and "approximately" rare.
- "for example" and "that is" written out; never "e.g." or "i.e." in text.
- "likely" more often than "probably", both used.
- Figure references lowercase panels: (Fig. 2c), (Fig. 2c,d), (Extended Data
  Fig. 4a–c), (Supplementary Fig. 3), (Supplementary Table 5).
- Statistics in full, inline, often several per sentence: (P = 3.2 × 10^−8),
  (odds ratio (OR) = 2.07, 95% confidence interval (CI) 0.99–4.40; Fisher's
  exact test), (n = 1,204), (Pearson's r = 0.62). Gene symbols as given.
- Semicolons are common (about one per 280 words), often separating
  parallel results inside one sentence: "RUNX2 and PAX9 for osteogenesis[36];
  MYOD1 for myogenesis[40]".
- "(Methods)" points to the Methods section; "(Supplementary Note 2)".

## Section rules

**Abstract**, ~155 words, ~7 sentences of ~23 words. Background or stake in
one sentence ("…, yet … remain poorly understood"), "Here we …" (usually no
comma), the dataset and its size with numbers ("across 463,134
individuals"), findings with counts ("17 independent variants"), and a
closing sentence on what the work shows or enables.

**Introduction**, ~120 words, ~5 sentences of ~25 words, three or four
citation groups per paragraph. Short paragraphs; each states what is known,
then the gap, often in a final sentence such as "Thus, a … is needed." or
"…, but the mechanisms … remain unclear."

**Results**, ~130 words, ~5 sentences averaging 27 words: long, data-dense
sentences, the most parenthetical prose of the four journals (about six
parentheses per paragraph: panels, statistics, sample sizes, abbreviations,
"(Methods)"). Openings: "To determine whether …, we …", "We then …",
"We also examined …", "Within the … compartment (258,618 cells), we …".
Endings: a sentence that states what the analysis shows, often with
"Thus," "Therefore," or "suggesting that …"; "Notably," "Finally,"
"Additionally," and "In contrast," are used more than in Nature, but still
less than once per paragraph.

**Discussion**, ~150 words, ~6 sentences of ~27 words, heavily hedged
(about two hedges per paragraph) and in the first person ("We found …",
"we demonstrated …"). Paragraphs often end on what remains to be tested
("… remains to be investigated.", "This will require future work …").

**Methods**, ~130 words, ~6 sentences of ~24 words; passive-heavy (about
four passive constructions per paragraph) but with "we" too; software,
versions and parameters inline ("(v.2.3.1; default parameters)").

## Tone

Quantitative and genetic: every claim is attached to a count, an effect size
or a test. Mechanistic language is cautious ("may act", "could indicate").
