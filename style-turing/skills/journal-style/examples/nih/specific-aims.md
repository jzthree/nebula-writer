# NIH: funded Specific Aims pages

Specific Aims pages from funded NIH applications that their authors shared openly under CC BY 4.0, reproduced with attribution (figures omitted). Read them for how the page moves from the problem to the hypothesis and the aims, how each aim is stated, and how the page closes; never copy their wording.

## Example 1 (R01 renewal)

Scientific research is most efficient when new hypotheses are based on the sum total of all human knowledge to date. Unfortunately, we as a research community do a relatively poor job of organizing existing biomedical knowledge. There are at least two key inefficiencies in our current system of knowledge management. First, our primary mechanism for communicating new findings continues to be unstructured, free-text publications. This corpus of documents is very difficult to utilize computationally (for example, to build tools to analyze and visualize relationships between biomedical entities), and therefore individual scientists are generally limited to the tiny proportion of knowledge contained in the papers they are personally able to read. Second, the landscape of biomedical knowledge is highly fragmented. This fragmentation of knowledge is observed in the > 1 million new research articles published every year, as well as among the hundreds of databases that aim to structure information in specific areas of biology.

This proposal addresses both of these challenges, focusing on making all biomedical knowledge Findable, Reusable, Accessible, and Interoperable (FAIR). We will continue our work leveraging Wikidata, a centralized and structured knowledge base that is openly edited and maintained by the community. Our vision to make Wikidata the largest repository of open biomedical knowledge can be achieved through the following three aims (Figure 1):

Aim #1: Build an ecosystem of tools that advances both the quantity and quality of biomedical data in Wikidata. Having accurate and complete information is essential to Wikidata's continued growth as a trusted knowledge source.
A) Enhance our data ingestion ecosystem for adding valuable sources of biomedical information to Wikidata. This work will address the entire data pipeline from an informatics, software, and legal perspective.
B) Create computable data models to ensure data quality and consistency. Based on Shape Expressions (ShEx), these data models will support robust data validation and surveillance tools.

Aim #2: Create Wikidata-based tools to facilitate and incentivize third-party data contributions. These tools are specifically aimed at convincing biomedical resource owners to contribute to Wikidata.
A) Extend our Wikidata Integrator (WDI) python library into a fully-featured database connector. WDI will support data modeling and validation checks, as well as advanced logging and reporting functionality.
B) Generate automated reports of community contributions to relevant Wikidata items. These reports will notify curators when additions or edits are made, effectively serving as a universal community curation interface.

Aim #3: Solicit "deep and narrow" contributions from targeted communities of domain experts. In contrast to the broad and shallow focus of most database imports, this aim will target information from experts that is not currently captured in any structured format.
A) Integrate our Wikidata-backed WikiGenomes application with the Apollo genome annotation tool. This synergy will combine our complementary strengths of a powerful user interface and robust community infrastructure.
B) Generalize the Chlambase application into a deployable Software Development Kit. This SDK will enable organism-specific genome databases to be created with minimal resources and administration effort.
C) Capture structured data from review article authors through our partnership with the journal GENE. This existing partnership utilizes a dual publication model to incentivize expert contributions.

Crossing all of these Specific Aims will be a Driving Biological Project focusing on infectious disease research. We will apply the tools and approaches developed in this proposal to build the knowledge management foundation for a project to detect pathogens from unbiased metagenomic sequence data.

Source: Andrew I. Su et al., "Gene Wiki: A community-maintained knowledge base of biomedical information", NIH R01 (2017), https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.6211181.v3, CC BY 4.0.

## Example 2 (NIDDK-funded research grant)

Obesity affects nearly a third of adult Americans with comorbidities that include some of the leading causes of death in Western societies. One major co-morbidity associated with obesity is liver disease caused by excessive lipid accumulation. However, there are major gaps in our knowledge with respect to the inter-relationships between obesity, insulin resistance and lipid metabolism. Whereas insulin normally promotes lipid storage, the fact that decreased insulin action associated with obesity does not result in reduced levels of lipids suggests that mechanisms exist which can maintain lipid levels even in insulin resistant states. This is an essential characteristic of the pathophysiology of obesity, and the molecular mechanisms underlying this are poorly understood. An emerging candidate to mediate lipid storage in insulin resistant states is the nutrient sensing kinase mTORC1. This proposal will test the hypothesis that mTORC1 activation in the obese state elevates lipid levels, due to activation of both adipogenesis and lipogenesis.

Aim 1: Determination of the role of mTORC1 in adipose tissue lipid homeostasis. Several lines of evidence support a central metabolic role of mTORC1 in regulating lipid metabolism in adipose tissues, though the effects of chronic mTORC1 activation in lean and obese animals have not been explored. We are the first to generate a mouse model which has chronic elevations in mTORC1 activity specifically in adipose tissue. We will utilize these mice to determine effects on lipid synthesis, breakdown and adipogenesis, as well as indirect effects of enhanced adipose mTORC1 activation on hepatic steatosis and whole-animal insulin sensitivity. These studies will determine how chronic activation of mTORC1 alters adipogenesis and lipogenesis. We will focus on the role of key mediators in these processes, SREBP1c and PPARγ, and mechanistically how they are regulated by mTORC1.

Aim 2: Identify the effects of modulating mTORC1 activity in liver tissue. Reducing mTORC1 activity in liver results in mice resistant to diet-induced hepatic steatosis. It is not clear whether this is also true for individuals with existing liver disease. We will induce or inhibit mTORC1 activity in adult mouse livers via adenoviral Cre expression, including in mice with pre-existing hepatic steatosis. These experiments will determine whether mTORC1 is necessary and sufficient for hepatic steatosis, whether inhibition of mTORC1 can alleviate hepatic steatosis and will explore the mechanisms by which mTORC1 regulates SREBP1c function.

Aim 3: Determine the physiological relevance of the SREBP1c feed forward loop with respect to obesity and fatty liver disease. A central mediator of both insulin and mTORC1-dependent lipogenesis is the transcription factor SREBP1c. A major feature of SREBP1c function is a positive feedback loop which further increases SREBP1c transcription, a process that is initiated by, among other factors, mTORC1. This feed-forward loop amplifies signaling through SREBP1c and may play a role in the difficulty in controlling unrestrained de novo lipogenesis. To test the role of this feedback loop in vivo we have targeted the endogenous Srebf1 locus in rats and have mutated the relevant upstream SRE. Without SREBP1c binding to its own promoter, these rats are expected to be refractory to its own positive feedback loop. Using these rats we will determine the role of this positive feedback of SREBP1c on adipogenesis, lipogenesis and insulin resistance. These studies will provide key insights into the contribution of this positive feedback loop to obesity and its co-morbidities.

Impact of the propose studies: These studies will answer fundamental questions relevant to the regulation of lipid homeostasis in both normal and pathophysiological states such as obesity by evaluating the role of mTORC1 in a tissue-specific manner in an intact rodent. These studies will provide a more complete mechanistic understanding of how mTORC1, PPARγ and SREBP1c contribute to the development of obesity, fatty liver disease and its treatment.

Source: Dave Bridges, "Regulation of Lipid Storage by mTORC1", NIDDK-funded application (2016), https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.3381487, CC BY 4.0.
