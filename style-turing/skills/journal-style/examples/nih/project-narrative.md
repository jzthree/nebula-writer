# NIH: funded Project Narratives

Project Narratives from funded NIH applications that their authors shared openly under CC BY 4.0, reproduced with attribution. Read them for rhythm, sentence shape, density and voice; never copy their wording into a new paragraph.

## Example 1 (F31 predoctoral fellowship)

Aging and age-related diseases are driven by epigenetic changes which may be reversible. There is strong evidence to suggest that the interaction of epigenomic elements called 'enhancers' and 'R-loops' may drive aging at the cellular level. By studying these interactions, we will expand our knowledge of epigenome aging and R-loops, while potentially revealing novel anti-aging therapeutic targets.

Source: Henry E. Miller, "The dynamics and impact of R-loops in epigenetic stability and aging", NIH F31 (2020), https://www.ogrants.org/grants/miller_henrye_2020, CC BY 4.0.

## Example 2 (R25 research education program)

Many important current problems in infectious disease control and prevention – such as the potential for test-and-treat programs to curtail HIV/AIDS transmission and the development of strategies for trachoma elimination – are best understood through a combination of mathematical models of infectious disease dynamics, well-designed field trials, and careful analysis of observational and experimental data. The International Clinics on Infectious Disease Dynamics and Data (ICI3D) program will provide a unique and much needed opportunity for junior researchers from the US and Africa to develop the toolsets necessary to conduct this type of research and to communicate their questions, methods, and findings across interdisciplinary boundaries.

Source: Juliet Pulliam and Alex Welte, "International Clinics on Infectious Disease Dynamics and Data", NIH R25 (2012), https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.6744221, CC BY 4.0.
